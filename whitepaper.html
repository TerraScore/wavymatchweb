Section One
What Wavy actually does — the honest version

Most dating apps are catalogues. You browse photos, swipe based on a bio, maybe message, and wait. If a plan ever happens, it happens later, off the app, after a lot of unpaid effort negotiating "when are you free."

Wavy skips the negotiation. You pick an activity, a time, and a spot, and send it. The other person taps one button. That's the whole ask. Or skip planning entirely — drop into a Spot or Event and see who's already there, or go live and get matched with someone nearby in seconds.

There are four things that make this work together:

The Core Mechanic

Plans

Pick an activity — badminton, chai, a bike ride, a movie, whatever — pick a time, pick a spot (or make one up), and send it. One button. The other person says yes or no. No back-and-forth, no group-chat logistics thread.

Section Two
Why this is different — and why it matters

The insight in one sentence: A sent Plan beats a matched profile, every time. A match is a maybe. A Plan is a time, a place, and a yes. Tinder gives you the maybe. Wavy gives you the yes.

Let's be honest about what the existing apps are. They're catalogues with a chat feature bolted on. You browse, you swipe, you hope the conversation doesn't die in the first three messages. Getting from there to an actual plan takes real, unpaid effort from at least one person — and most of the time, nobody does it.

Wavy skips straight to the plan. Instead of hoping a match eventually turns into plans, the first thing you can send is the plan itself.

Three specific things make this hard for anyone else to copy:

A Plan pre-commits both sides to something real. When you match on a swipe app, neither of you has agreed to anything beyond finding each other's photos acceptable. When someone accepts a Plan, they've agreed to an actual activity, at an actual time. That's a fundamentally different level of intent — and it's why Plans convert to real meetups at a rate no match ever will.

Spots & Events remove the cold-start problem entirely. Instead of messaging a total stranger from zero context, you can see exactly who's already at a venue, or already going to an event, before you say a single word. The venue does the pre-qualifying that an algorithm can only guess at.

Live Match creates urgency that's actually real. Not the manufactured urgency of "this match expires in 24 hours" — real urgency, because the person is genuinely nearby and available right now, this minute. That changes the psychology completely. It's not "maybe we'll meet someday." It's "are you free right now, yes or no."

Section Five
How to actually get people to use it

The constraint nobody wants to say out loud: Wavy has a density problem. If nobody else is at a spot, there's nothing to join and nobody to send a Plan to. The go-to-market plan must solve density first — not distribution. Getting a million downloads in ten cities doesn't work. Getting 500 daily active users in one city does. Then it becomes self-sustaining.

Foundation — Done

The product works. Everything is shipped.

Matching, Plans, Spots & Events, Live Match, chat, subscriptions, notifications, selfie verification — all live. 92 database migrations, 18 edge functions. The infrastructure can handle real users. This is the starting line, not the finish.

Phase 1 — Now to 3 months

Own 1–2 cities. Don't be everywhere.

Pick two high-density metros with strong café, gym, and event culture — the kind of cities where people already have active social lives they want to extend, not create from scratch. Then:

  • Run "Wavy Nights" — curated events where 80–120 users all join the same spot and send Plans to each other. These generate the first wave of genuine success stories
  • Sign 20 venue partners who create permanent spots, offered 3 months of free Featured status in exchange
  • Campus rep program: pay 10 students per university $200/month to host weekly Wavy spots in the library, gym, common areas
  • Target: 500 DAU per city before any paid marketing. Density before distribution
Phase 2 — Months 2–6

Make "we made a Plan on Wavy" a thing people say

Content is the marketing. Real stories from real people beat any ad creative. The goal here is to find the first dozen people whose Plan actually happened and make their story shareable.

  • TikTok / Reels: "Wavy Spot Diaries" — genuine accounts of Plans that actually happened. No actors, no scripts
  • Partner with 3–5 mid-tier lifestyle influencers (50k–500k followers) to host Wavy Spots at events they attend
  • UGC incentive: share a screenshot of a Plan that actually happened and get 1 month of Pro free
  • Weekly "What's happening near you" push digest — editorial framing, not a notification dump
Phase 3 — Months 4–9

Venues start paying because the data speaks for itself

After density is proven, the Featured Spot pitch becomes easy. The deck is a single slide: "17 Plans were sent from your café last month."

  • Launch Featured Spot paid tier with proper self-serve onboarding
  • Pitch 5 events / festivals on powered Spots for their attendees
  • Begin Wavy for Teams pilot: 3 beta companies, HR-facing
  • Referral program live: invite 3 friends, get 2 weeks Pro free
Phase 4 — Month 6 onwards

Only then: performance marketing and new cities

Paid acquisition only makes sense when the product can retain users. That means D30 retention above 18% and a proven free-to-Pro conversion path. Before that, paid ads are just buying churn. After that, they're buying growth.

  • Meta and TikTok ads using UGC testimonial creative — the "we made a Plan and it actually happened" format
  • Expand to new cities only once the first two are profitable at the cohort level
  • ASO and editorial pitches to Apple / Google once traction data supports the ask

Section Six
What to build next, and why

Every feature on this list was chosen by asking one question: does this keep users coming back, make them more likely to pay, or make the network denser? If it doesn't do at least one of those three things, it's not on the list.

01

Retention — these fix the business first

Build these before anything else

Spot & Event Stories — 24-hour photos inside a spot

Users post a photo from inside an active spot or event, visible only to attendees for 24 hours. This does three things at once: creates FOMO for people who weren't there, drives repeat visits to the same venues, and generates organic content that markets the app without any spend. It's Instagram Stories, but hyperlocal and ephemeral in a way that actually matters.

"Going Tonight" — pre-announce your arrival at a venue

Users mark that they'll be at a specific place at a specific time, no invite required. Nearby users see it. This is the passive, no-commitment cousin of a Plan — you're not asking anyone specific, you're just marking that you're up for something if someone bites. It's the feature that gives people a reason to open Wavy at 5pm every day.

Mutual Spot History badge on profiles

"You've both been to the same climbing gym three times." The RPC for this already exists (get_user_spot_history). It needs a UI card on the profile view — probably a day of build. The impact is outsized because it tells a story about compatibility that no algorithm score can. Shared real experiences are the strongest social signal.

Streak for consecutive days with a spot visit

A Duolingo-style streak counter. Free users see it; Pro users get one streak freeze per month. Simple to build, effective at driving daily active usage, and a direct upsell hook into the subscription. Gamification doesn't have to be complex to work — it just has to create a number people don't want to lose.

Smarter re-engagement pushes — contextual, not generic

Instead of "You have new matches," send: "The person you sent a Plan to last week is at [Blueprint Coffee] right now." Contextual, specific nudges tied to a real place and a real Plan are meaningfully better than generic ones. The location and Plan data to make this work is already in the system.

02

Social expansion — these grow the top of funnel

Build once retention is above target

Group Plan — invite everyone in the Spot at once

One button: "Send this Plan to the whole room." Instead of picking one person to invite, the Plan goes out to everyone currently in the Spot, and whoever taps yes is in. It removes the pressure of singling someone out while still getting an actual plan on the calendar. The existing invite-and-accept logic handles the rest.

Friend Bring-Along

Invite a real-world friend to join the same spot — a group of two entering together. This unlocks a completely different use case: "night out with your friend, you both meet people and make plans." The addressable market immediately broadens from solo singles to friend groups on a night out. This is probably the feature with the highest potential to escape the "dating app" category.

In-Spot mini-games — Trivia, Icebreaker, Speed Round

Nobody else is building shared, real-time games inside a physical venue. A quick round of Trivia or an Icebreaker question gives people something to do together before anyone has to send a Plan cold — it lowers the bar for that first invite considerably. This is a forward-looking build, not something to promise as shipped.

03

Monetisation infrastructure

Build as B2B revenue becomes real

Venue partner dashboard

A simple web dashboard showing venue partners how many Wavy users visited their spot this week, peak hours, and how many Plans were sent from their venue. This data makes the Featured Spot subscription sell itself. "17 Plans were sent from your café last month" is more powerful than any sales pitch.

Queue Boost as consumable IAP

$2.99 for 30 minutes at the top of the Live Match queue. No subscription needed. Runs on existing IAP infrastructure. Target: Friday/Saturday evenings, impulse purchases. Each sale is also a nudge toward the Pro subscription, which costs the same per week.

Gift a month of Pro

Let users send a friend a month of Wavy Pro. Every gift is a new paid user acquisition at zero CAC — the gifter pays, the recipient becomes a user, and if the product works, they stay. The social signal ("my friend thinks I should be on this") is also a better acquisition mechanism than any ad creative.
